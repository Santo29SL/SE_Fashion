The Southern Edit
Fashion Technology Project
The Southern Edit is a fashion e-commerce and editorial website centered on the craft traditions of four Dravidian regions: Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, and Andhra & Telangana. It explores how traditional textile crafts and regional design knowledge can live on through contemporary clothing and accessories made from salvaged, surplus, and reclaimed materials.

The website combines fashion retail with cultural storytelling. Visitors can explore regional craft traditions, browse products by season or region, read about materials and techniques, and view products from multiple angles.
Project Purpose
Many traditional crafts depend on skilled makers, local materials, and communities of practice. When fewer people wear or buy products made with those crafts, the knowledge and livelihoods behind them can be at risk.

The Southern Edit presents a fashion model that connects those traditions with contemporary use. It focuses on:
- Giving discarded, surplus, or overlooked cloth a new use.
- Connecting contemporary products to regional textile and craft traditions.
- Making the origins and materials of products visible to shoppers.
- Presenting craft as part of everyday dressing rather than only as ceremonial or museum display.
The Four Regional Realms
The site organizes its cultural content around four regional realms. Each realm brings together places, crafts, materials, and products associated with that region.
- Tamil Nadu — The Temple & Loom Realm: Includes traditions such as Kanchipuram silk, Bhavani handloom, Athangudi tile motifs, and Madras checks.
- Kerala — The Kora & Kasavu Realm: Features kora cotton, kasavu borders, and handloom traditions associated with Kerala.
- Karnataka — The Chalukyan Stone & Silk Realm: Connects Mysore silk, Deccani wool, Kasuti embroidery, and the region’s architectural heritage.
- Andhra & Telangana — The Yarn-Dye & Pen-Lore Realm: Highlights Pochampally ikat, Pedana block printing, and Srikalahasti Kalamkari.
Each realm has its own visual identity, descriptive copy, craft references, and associated products. The state or regional name is also presented in its regional language.
Products and Materials
Products are presented as contemporary fashion pieces connected to craft and material reuse. The catalog includes clothing, accessories, bags, and other pieces, with product details such as:
- Product name and description.
- Region and craft association.
- Salvaged or surplus material source.
- Material, hardware, and origin details.
- Available styling suggestions and colourways.
- Price, rating, and stock information.
- Product images and alternate views.
The product catalog is grouped into five collections: Summer, Fall, Winter, Spring, and International. Shoppers can browse the full collection or filter products by season, realm, audience, and sorting preference.
Website Sections
Home — Realms and the Revival
The landing page introduces the project’s central idea: traditional craft can be carried forward through new products made from existing material. A featured garment is presented with visual details that connect parts of the piece to the cloth, craft, or region behind them.

The regional sections introduce the four realms, their craft traditions, and the materials being revived. Visitors can explore a realm’s products or open its lookbook.
Shop — Seasonal Collections
The Shop organizes the catalog by season. Visitors can browse all products or select a seasonal collection. Filters allow browsing by audience and regional realm, while sorting options help visitors explore the product range.

Product cards link to quick views with additional product information and multiple product images.
Journal — Stories and Craft Vocabulary
The Journal presents editorial stories about regional crafts, textile techniques, materials, and artisan communities. A glossary explains selected craft terms, helping visitors understand the language used across the site.
Heritage — Materials and Provenance
The Heritage section explains the project’s approach to salvaged material, revived craft, modular design, and value for artisan communities. It also presents craft clusters and supply-chain information.
Lookbooks and Product Viewing
Realm lookbooks present products as a visual collection connected to a particular region. Product quick views include a Lookbook tab with a drag-enabled viewer for switching between image angles.

These views are intended to make product details easier to examine and to give the online shopping experience a more tactile, editorial quality.
Shopping Features
The website includes a shared shopping experience across pages:
- Add products and accessories to a persistent bag.
- Adjust item quantities and review the bag.
- View prices in the selected currency.
- Open product quick views and realm lookbooks.
- Receive on-site notifications for shopping actions.
The bag and currency selection are shared across the site rather than being separate on each page.
Design Approach
The visual design combines an editorial fashion-magazine style with a restrained palette inspired by natural cloth, dyes, metal, and regional materials. Large serif headlines, fine rules, product photography, captions, and scrolling ribbons give the pages a catalog-like rhythm.

The design aims to balance two kinds of information:
1. Fashion presentation: showing garments and accessories as contemporary products.
2. Cultural context: explaining the craft, place, and material connected to each piece.
Technology Overview
The project is built as a responsive web application using:
- React for interface components.
- TypeScript for typed product, realm, and shopping data.
- TanStack Router for page routing.
- Vite for development and production builds.
- CSS for the site’s editorial layout, interactions, and responsive styles.
- Local and hosted image assets for product, editorial, and regional imagery.
Core shopping rules—such as catalog filtering, season grouping, bag calculations, and price formatting—are kept in shared utility modules so they can be maintained separately from page presentation.
Accessibility and Responsive Design
The site uses semantic sections, headings, buttons, and labels to support navigation and interaction. Product images include descriptive alternative text where appropriate, while decorative images are hidden from assistive technology.

Layouts adapt to different screen sizes so visitors can explore the realms, shop collections, and use the product viewer on desktop and mobile screens.
Learning Outcomes
The project demonstrates how fashion technology can bring together:
- E-commerce and product discovery.
- Editorial storytelling and visual merchandising.
- Regional craft and material provenance.
- Reuse and circular-fashion concepts.
- Interactive product presentation.
- Shared cart state and responsive interface design.
Summary
The Southern Edit is a digital fashion concept that presents regional craft traditions through contemporary products made with salvaged and surplus materials. Its website combines a seasonal shop, four regional realms, editorial stories, lookbooks, and product-viewing tools to connect each product with the material and craft behind it.
